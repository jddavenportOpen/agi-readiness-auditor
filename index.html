AGI Readiness Auditor

Pressure-test a lab's public AGI-safety story against a published readiness framework. Sample report below: OpenAI vs OpenAI's own 5 Levels of AGI.

This audits public claims only. It can't see what a lab does internally. A high score means the public story is complete against the chosen framework. It doesn't mean the company is safe or that it honors its commitments. Every finding cites a source URL so you can check it yourself. Level 2: Reasoners substantial

Bar: Publicly claims reasoning-class models AND publishes evaluations of reasoning reliability, hallucination, or where the reasoning breaks.

OpenAI's o1 system card reports chain-of-thought reasoning evaluations and notes the model can still produce confident errors; reasoning reliability is evaluated but not guaranteed. source: https://openai.com/index/openai-o1-system-card/

Gap: Establishes reasoning-class models (o1) with reliability evaluations, but lacks explicit cross-domain human-level problem solving without tools and does not comprehensively address hallucination in reasoning contexts.

Level 3: Agents partial

Bar: Publicly addresses agentic risk: autonomy controls, action-space limits, oversight / human-in-the-loop, or a stated policy on autonomous action.

OpenAI's Preparedness Framework tracks Cybersecurity, CBRN, Persuasion, and Model Autonomy as risk categories, each scored Low/Medium/High/Critical with deployment gated above High. source: https://openai.com/index/updating-our-preparedness-framework/
OpenAI ships agentic products (Operator, agent mode) and states it applies oversight, confirmation steps, and constrained action spaces, but publishes limited detail on autonomous-action stop conditions. source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-operator/

Gap: No published detail on autonomous-action stop conditions, specific autonomy controls, or human-in-the-loop requirements / risk-gating thresholds for agentic systems.

Level 4: Innovators partial

Bar: Has a public position on AI-driven R&D acceleration risk, including dual-use scientific knowledge (e.g. bio/chem uplift).

On Innovators-level / scientific uplift, OpenAI's CBRN track addresses bio uplift to novices, but there is no standalone public framework for AI-accelerated R&D risk beyond the CBRN category. source: https://openai.com/index/updating-our-preparedness-framework/

Gap: CBRN track addresses bio uplift but lacks a standalone public position on AI-driven R&D acceleration broadly (chemistry, materials, other domains) and no stop-condition for Innovators-level invention capability.

Level 5: Organizations partial

Bar: Has a public governance position on highly autonomous / economically transformative systems: control, alignment guarantees, or a stated stop/pause condition.

OpenAI's Safety Advisory Group (SAG) reviews Preparedness scorecards and can recommend the leadership block deployment; the board retains oversight authority. source: https://openai.com/index/updating-our-preparedness-framework/
OpenAI's 'Planning for AGI and Beyond' commits in principle to caution and coordination as systems approach transformative capability, but the most concrete stop conditions are tied to Preparedness thresholds rather than the Level 5 tier. source: https://openai.com/index/planning-for-agi-and-beyond/

Gap: No public claim specifies a governance position, control mechanism, alignment guarantee, or stop/pause condition explicitly tied to systems capable of doing the work of an entire organization (Level 5).

How it works (and what it does NOT claim)

The CLI encodes real, citable frameworks (OpenAI 5 Levels, OpenAI Preparedness, Anthropic RSP) as rubrics. Each criterion has a specific bar and a source URL. It gathers a company's public claims, maps claims→criteria with a skeptical, evidence-bound LLM (cites only provided claims, never invents quotes, and falls back to a deterministic keyword match if no LLM key is set), and scores whether the public posture meets each bar. It does not measure internal practice or whether commitments are honored.
